# Interpretations (M3 geometry, M4 match rules)

Every place where the software had to pick a reading of the rules. "Confirmed" rows were checked against the Digital Rulebook 1.0.2 page image; "provisional" rows are Trench-Sim choices that an official ruling or playtesting may change. Wording is paraphrased. M4 Slice A rows (C9 to C14, U30 to U45) are at the end of each table. Rulebook wording discrepancies and their rulings are in `docs/rules-notes.md`.

## Provisional (Trench-Sim choices)
| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| U1 | Lengths are integers in thousandths of an inch; 1 mm = 0.03937" rounded to nearest thousandth (25.4 mm per inch). |
| U2 | The base footprint stands in for the model body in line of sight (the body cannot be separated from its base in 2D). *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U3 | Other models never block line of sight. |
| U4 | "As wide as its base": the terrain's extent across the attacker-target line is at least the base diameter. |
| U5 | "Between and partially blocking": at least one sight ray crosses the piece (a Wall must also obstruct that ray). *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U6 | Battlefield edge and terrain "more than half" tests use an exact bracketed circle-versus-rectangle method; where it cannot decide within about 0.001" it refuses (conservative). |
| U7 | The move-end clause ("no further from the start than Movement") is treated as already covered by the path-length limit. |
| U8 | Difficult terrain doubles the part of a leg inside it, proportionally for a partial crossing. |
| U9 | Substantial (Wall) terrain on the ground cannot be entered or crossed, because climbing is out of scope. |
| U10 | A path may cross a friendly base but may not end overlapping one. |
| U11 | Down models obstruct like any other base. |
| U12 | Movement cost: each leg is rounded up to a millionth of an inch, the legs are added, and the total is rounded up once to the next thousandth. A path never costs less than its true length, a single leg of exactly the allowance is legal, and splitting a path into many short legs changes the cost by at most about three millionths of an inch per leg, never downward. The cost is held in an Int128 and is reported clamped to the largest Length, so an absurdly long path is refused as too long rather than throwing. (An earlier plan rounded every leg up to a thousandth, which charged 0.600" for 300 diagonal legs of 0.001" whose true total is 0.4243"; the test `ManyShortLegsCostTheSameAsOneLongLegToTheThousandth` records the comparison.) |
| U13 | A ray that only grazes an edge or corner does not count as crossing. |
| U14 | Terrain heights are replaced by profiles chosen at setup: Low (under 1/2"), Cover (1/2" to 1"), Wall (substantial). The thresholds come from pp. 38-39; assigning real terrain to a profile is the scenario author's job. |
| U15 | "On or in contact": the target's base touches or overlaps the piece's footprint. |
| U16 | Sample points on the target are a fixed set (centre and facing rim points); the sampler is replaceable. *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U17 | High ground: more than half the base is within a walkable High-surface block (lowest id wins). |
| U18 | Walkable platforms may be entered and left anywhere along their footprint at no extra cost; no climbing, jumping or falling. |
| U19 | A platform never obstructs sight from or to a model standing on it. "Standing on it" means more than half the base is on it (high ground) or the base centre is strictly inside its footprint (a ground model overlapping a corner); touching or overlapping its edge from outside does not count. |
| U20 | An attacker with high ground is not obstructed by Ground-surface Walls (XCOM-style gameplay abstraction, not 3D). *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U21 | Targets on high ground get no extra shelter from ground attackers; sight is asymmetric by design. |
| U22 | Cover and line of sight share one obstruction rule; a piece that does not obstruct gives no cover as a Wall. *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U23 | Cover-profile pieces give cover to attackers on either level, when the cover conditions hold. *Since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25): replaced by U77/U78.* |
| U24 | The elevation attack bonus is not implemented in M3. |
| U25 | Deployment: a base must lie wholly within its zone (touching the edge is allowed). The "wholly within" wording comes from text extraction of p. 146 and was not separately confirmed against the page image. |
| U26 | Difficult terrain doubling ("every 1\" counts as 2\"") comes from the keyword text, not a visually checked page. Dangerous terrain has no effect in M3 (the flag exists only). |
| U27 | Leaving the battlefield is checked at the waypoints only. The set of base positions with more than half the base on the table is convex, so a straight leg between two legal waypoints cannot dip below half. |
| U28 | Cover from a Cover-profile piece needs a sight ray to cross it, and rays end on the target, so a model standing inside the piece gets cover even if most of the piece lies behind it, and a piece merely touching the far side of the base does not. |
| U29 | CheckPath does not validate the mover's starting position or cap the number of waypoints; callers (the M4 command layer) own both. The start-within-1\" test uses the mover's current position, and retreat is checked by the caller with the retreat flag (Slice B). |
| U30 | A Down model stands up automatically, as an event right after its activation starts, rather than as an action it chooses. Halved Movement is rounded down to a thousandth and limits both Move and Dash. |
| U31 | The Down penalty is -1 DICE on Success Rolls taken for a Down model (the reading that agrees with both chapters, question 2). A Down model always stands before acting, so in Slice A the penalty never changes a roll. |
| U32 | The Claim No Man's Land battlefield is a fixture: 36" x 36" table, 6" deep zones along the west and east edges (24" apart), five 4" x 4" Cover rubble Objective pieces centred on the printed marker positions (6" in from each zone edge and the table edge, plus the midpoint), and extra non-Objective walls and rough ground. The book prints none of the table size, zone depth or terrain; the terrain roll-off and set-up are not played. |
| U33 | Blood Marker spending is a pause with no dice rolled: before the Success Roll of a Dash or Shoot by a model holding markers (the opponent chooses 0 to all), and before an Injury Roll against a model holding markers (the attacker chooses 0 to all). Nothing is asked when there is nothing to spend. Spent markers leave the model at once. |
| U34 | An Out of Action model is taken off the table (no position) and keeps its marker count, which nothing reads. |
| U35 | The elevated-attacker +1 DICE (3" or more higher, p. 43) is not applied. M3's discrete High level cannot measure 3"; each attack declaration records both models' levels so the bonus can be added later without changing the log format. |
| U36 | "On" an Objective piece means more than half the base is within it; "in or within 1\"" means the nearest point of the base is at most 1" from the piece. Down models count; Out of Action models are off the table and do not. If exactly one side has a model on the piece, that side controls it; otherwise the larger count of models on or within 1" does, and equal counts control nothing. |
| U37 | A Shaken warband's attack Success Rolls are Risky, like its Dashes. A failed Risky shot is a miss and then ends the activation. |
| U38 | (Replaced in Slice B by C21 and U51-U52.) In Slice A shooting into melee was refused as unsupported. |
| U39 | "Models on the battlefield" for the elimination win means deployed models that are not Out of Action. The win is checked right after each Injury Roll. |
| U40 | Equal Victory Points after the final Turn are a draw (the book says "more Victory Points" wins and is silent on a tie). The outcome reason is VictoryPoints with no winner. |
| U41 | Objectives are scored once per Turn, after the Morale Phase and before the Turn ends; a Turn in which a warband flees ends the match unscored. |
| U42 | A weapon's range must be an even number of thousandths of an inch so that half the range, the Short/Long boundary, is exact. |
| U43 | A Move or Dash path has at most 16 waypoints (command size limit, checked before any geometry). |
| U44 | In a match with a battlefield, models are set up one at a time with PlaceModel at a point (base wholly within the zone, using M3 placement). DeployModel is refused there; PlaceModel is refused without a battlefield. |
| U45 | Slice A models have one ranged weapon. Weapon and Armour appear only as flat injury modifiers (Armour 0 to -3, weapon -3 to +3), combined by the M1 rule that negatives total at most -3. |
| U46 | **Replaced by U57 to U65.** The Slice B first cut used a single straight line from the charger to the target; the rules ask for the shortest available route (p. 37), so that was an implementation gap, not the intended rule. Difficult terrain still costs double, so the same Charge Bonus buys less distance. |
| U47 | Contact between two round bases is not a whole number of thousandths, so "stops as soon as it is in contact" is read as: the last point (to a thousandth) that does not overlap the target. The model finishes within about 0.002" of touching. The optional "carry on if it stays in contact and finishes within 1" of other enemies" is not used. |
| U48 | Charge bonus and halving: after standing up the whole total (Movement plus Charge Bonus, capped at 12") is halved, rounded down to a thousandth. The die is rolled only after the declaration is accepted; a charge that falls short, or cannot move at all because a friendly base blocks the line, is a legal outcome, not a rejection. The friendly-base handling is now U59 (the straight-line "stops short" wording is replaced). |
| U49 | A Risky Success Roll that fails during a free attack just misses: only the active model has an activation to end. |
| U50 | Retreat details. The path is fixed when the Retreat is declared and must be legal then (no illegal path is ever accepted and then abandoned). The opponent is asked after each free attack whether to attack with another eligible model or decline the rest. Eligible: a standing enemy model with a Melee Weapon within 1" of the retreating model, not yet used. Down enemy models cannot fight. If every eligible model has attacked or the retreater is Out of Action the sequence ends. A Retreat for which no legal path exists cannot be taken at all (so no free attacks occur), because the rules say the model then cannot move. Free attacks continue against a model already taken Down by an earlier one (+1 INJURY DICE, C20). The retreat path may leave the 1" of the enemies the model started within, but not enter that of others or cross a base. |
| U51 | Shooting into melee: the target must still be legal (Line of Sight, range) for the intended enemy model. On a 1-3 the shooter picks one of its own models within 1" of the enemy target (any standing or Down friendly, never itself). Long Range and Cover are then worked out for that friendly model from the positions; Line of Sight and range do not have to hold for it. The D6 comes before the Blood Marker pause for the Success Roll, so the opponent chooses knowing the real target. |
| U52 | A forced shot at a friendly model: the opponent may still spend the shooter's markers for -1 DICE on the Success Roll (p. 28). Nobody may spend the friendly model's markers on the Injury Roll, because the rule gives that choice to the opponent of the model's owner, who does not make the roll. |
| U53 | Defended Obstacle uses the M3 Cover result: the target is in cover from that attacker and the piece providing it is the one the cover calculation reports. |
| U54 | One Melee Weapon per model: no Off-Hand attacks, no Multiple Melee Weapons, CLEAVE, BLOCK, Fear, Diving Charge or ASSAULT. A model's melee injury modifier is flat, like its ranged one. |
| U55 | Down models are measured from their bases only. The note on p. 49 allows measuring to or from any part of a Down model; that is not modelled (base measurement is the more conservative reading for range and engagement). |
| U56 | The -1 DICE for Success Rolls taken for a Down model (p. 49) cannot arise: Down models cannot fight or shoot, and stand up before acting. |
| U57 | Charge route: the planner finds a route of the charger's centre to contact with the chosen target, round Impassable terrain, enemy bases and (when they cannot be crossed) friendly bases, that is the shortest to within the tolerance in U58. On open ground it is the old straight line. That the route is the shortest is the rule (p. 37); the geometric treatment below is an implementation convention. The planner does **not** guarantee the mathematically shortest route: it is a deterministic approximation with the stated bounds. |
| U58 | Curves and tangents: the forbidden region round each base or block is approximated by a 32-sided polygon (fixed integer trig table, circumscribed, with a small margin), so a route never cuts a corner the real outline would forbid (every route is legal by the exact movement test, and was also checked against plain Euclidean distances in the audit tests). Routes are longer than the true shortest by up to about 0.33% of each arc wrapped, plus a few thousandths per corner; the tests allow 0.6% + 0.012" against a finely sampled brute force and measured at most 0.016" over 1,500 random scenes. A doorway (gap between two obstacles) narrower than the base is never used; one wider by up to about 0.01" may also count as closed (measured 0.006" in the audit). Contact with the target is sought at 128 fixed points round it plus the direct line; the end can be up to about 0.005" off the true optimum. Difficult terrain: its extra cost is counted in the route's cost, but the planner adds no vertices round Difficult terrain, so it picks the cheapest route among those that go through corners of Impassable terrain and friendly bases; it does not bend a route round a patch of Difficult ground (see rules-notes, open question 5). |
| U59 | Friendly models: a route is first planned through friends (crossing is allowed only if the model can move entirely past, p. 35, which the movement check enforces). If that route is illegal it is replanned with friends as blocks; if that fails too the charge stops short of the friend that is in the way. |
| U60 | Equal-cost routes: when two routes tie, the one whose waypoints come first in (X, Y) order wins. The choice does not depend on the order in which obstacles were supplied. |
| U61 | Interposition (p. 36) is judged on the planned route, not the centre-to-centre line: a charge is refused if that route passes within 1" of an enemy other than the target before the charger is within 1" of the target. The planner does not detour to avoid the 1" zones of other enemies; it takes the shortest route and then judges it. |
| U62 | Search extent. Declaring a charge needs a target in sight within 12" (p. 36), and nothing in the rules caps the length of the route. The search therefore covers the whole table, and a route much longer than 12" is a legal declaration: the model moves as far along it as its Movement allows and the charge fails. The 12" cap is on what a charge can *move* (Movement plus Charge Bonus), not on the routes considered. A declaration is refused as "no route" only when the target cannot be reached at all, or when the part of its route that 12" of Movement covers would need more than the 64 legs of U63. (An earlier version of this row, from the first routing change, searched only routes of 12" or less to contact; that wrongly refused valid charges, including some whose first 12" would have ended within 1" of the target. Superseded in the audit.) |
| U63 | The recorded charge path has at most 64 legs (`Charging.MaxLegs`): the path is the part of the route that the charge's Movement covers, at most 12" of Movement (less distance where Difficult terrain doubles the cost). A shortest route turns at most once round each obstacle (at most 17 legs round a 32-gon, 8 round a corner of terrain), so this is ample for any real table, but it is not proved: `CheckRoute` counts the legs that 12" of Movement covers on the route it found and refuses the declaration if they exceed 64. `Plan` applies the same limit to the way round friendly bases (U65) and to the stop-short path: a way round that would need more than 64 legs counts as no way round, so the model stops short of the friend. No plan of more than 64 legs can therefore reach `ChargeMoved` after the die is rolled. (Before the reducer-hardening change the way round was not checked; a test scene that weaves through rows of friends gives 65 to 73 legs at reaches of 19.25" and more. A charge's reach is at most 12", so this was never hit in play.) The path is not part of the state hash. |
| U64 | The reducer replans every `ChargeMoved` from the prior state and the recorded roll and requires the same path, cost and reached flag. A forged path, or one that is legal but is not the route the planner chooses (U57 to U60, so possibly longer, or the same length by a different way), is rejected. |
| U65 | Friends and long detours: a friendly base is passable on the route first planned, and the model may cross it only if it can move entirely past (p. 35). If the die cannot carry the model past (so it would end on, or part-way across, a friend), the route is replanned with friendly bases as obstacles and the model follows that route, however much longer it is; with no such route it stops short of the friend. A stop within 1" of the target counts as a successful charge (p. 37) even though the bases are not in contact. Open: whether a model should walk a long way round (even away from the target) rather than stop short; see rules-notes, open question 6. |
| U66 | Climbing a ladder costs the tower's height in Movement, on top of the distance the base moves (the 2r step from the foot over the edge to the top). The p. 40 example counts a 3" climb against the move; the text does not state it as a rule. Going down costs the same. (Towers proposal, stage T2.) |
| U67 | A model on a tower stands wholly on its platform or wholly off the tower: a move on the platform keeps the whole base on it, and on the ground the tower is solid. The book only says a climber "cannot finish the move halfway up a wall" (p. 39). |
| U68 | A tower is entered and left by its ladders, or (stage T6) by climbing straight up or down a side with a Risky Success Roll (U75). Jumping down (p. 40) is not built, so it is refused rather than ruled on; this is a limitation of this version, not a reading of the rules. Models are placed (deployed) on the ground, never on a tower. |
| U69 | Towers (towers proposal, 2026-10-20). A tower is a walkable High platform with a `Climb`: a height and ladders. Its height must be over 1" (terrain up to 1" is Open and needs no ladder, p. 39) and under 3" (from 3" the Elevated position bonus, p. 43, and Falling, p. 41, would apply, and neither is built). The two towers in the tower layout are 2" high, so no rule for 3" or more is needed. |
| U70 | Distances between models at different heights are measured in three dimensions, between the nearest points of the two bases, each a flat disc at its elevation (pp. 30, 43 say "nearest points" and "closest point of the base"; the book shows no example across heights). Models at the same height are measured exactly as before. Consequence: a model on a 2" tower is never within 1" of a model on the ground, so there is no engagement, melee or charge contact between them, and ranges across heights are slightly longer than on the table. |
| U71 | A ladder is a point on one side of its tower: a model climbs there, from the foot (base touching the side from outside, on the ground) to the top (base touching the same side from inside, on the platform). The book gives no ladder width; the width drawn by the client is decoration. Ladders are Open terrain (p. 39), so climbing one needs no roll. |
| U72 | A charge between heights (up or down a ladder) is not built in this version and is refused at declaration, before the Charge Bonus is rolled, with its own reason. The book would allow it (ladders are Open terrain, p. 39). Charges on one platform, and on the ground around the towers, work as before; from the ground a tower is solid to the charge planner, and on a platform the route stays wholly on it. Only models at the charger's height are obstacles to its route. |
| U73 | Sight and cover over towers use the existing readings unchanged: a tower blocks sight between two ground models like a wall, never blocks sight to or from a model on it (U19), a model on it sees over ground walls (U20), and it gives a model on it no extra shelter (U21, U22, U23). Height alone decides nothing else: the Elevated position bonus (3" or more, p. 43) is not reached at 2". *Since line-of-sight stage L2: sight over towers follows the heights (U77, U78).* |
| U74 | Objective control is measured like everything else (U70): a model on a 2" tower is never on, in or within 1" of a ground Objective piece, so it never counts towards controlling one. |
| U75 | Climbing a sheer surface (p. 39) is built for tower sides (stage T6). The book's "moves within 1\" of a sheer surface" is read as the base touching the side: the climb is one leg straight across the side, from the base touching it on the ground to the base touching it from inside on the platform (or back), anywhere along a side where the whole base fits on the platform. It costs what a ladder climb costs (the step plus the height, U66), and the whole move, climb included, must fit the model's Movement ("enough movement to clear the entire surface"). A Risky Success Roll is taken; the opponent may spend the climber's Blood Markers on it as on any Success Roll (pp. 27-28). The whole move is declared first and the roll is made before the model moves: on a Success it makes the whole move; on a Failure it moves only as far as where the climb starts (if it is not there already) and its activation ends. A ladder leg is never a side climb and needs no roll. |
| U76 | Limits of the side climb in this version: at most one per Move or Dash path (a Dash that climbs rolls for the Dash first, then for the climb); none in a Retreat or a Charge (charges across heights stay refused, U72); and the point where the climb starts must not overlap another model's base, because a failed roll leaves the model there. The two ground wall pieces have no height, so they stay solid and are not climbable (U9). |
| U77 | Height-based sight (line-of-sight plan, stage L1, 2026-10-25; the rules' sight since stage L2: shots, forced shots, charges, Fight, cover, and the client's previews). The book prints no heights, so: a figure is 1.2" tall; the shooter's eye is 1.1" above its base centre; nine points on the target, at 0.3", 0.7" and 1.1" above its base (legs, body, head), each left, middle and right, set across the line at 0.8 of the base radius (the base is not part of the model, p. 30). Terrain is a box from the ground to its height: a tower's climb height, else Low 0.25", Cover 0.75", Wall 3" (the heights the client draws). A ray is blocked when it passes through the inside of a box; grazing does not count (as U13); other models never block (U3); the platform a model stands on never blocks it (U19). A model on a non-tower High platform stands on the platform's height. The shortcut U20 has no place here: a raised eye sees over a piece only if the piece is low enough. Known limit: a slit narrower than the spacing of the points can hide a target the table would show a sliver of, and the side points at 0.8 of the radius see less round a corner than today's rim points. `docs/line-of-sight.md` lists how the ten ruled scenes changed. |
| U78 | Cover since line-of-sight stage L2 (2026-10-25), all four tests of p. 38 checked separately on the height-based sight (U77): the piece is at least 1/2" high (Cover 0.75", Wall 3", a tower its height; Low 0.25" never); the target's base touches or overlaps it (U15); it is as wide as the base across the line (U4); and it hides at least one of the target's nine points while the target is still partly seen (Partial). A target seen in full has no cover, a target not seen cannot be targeted, and a ray merely crossing a piece gives nothing. The platform a model stands on never hides it (U19), so it is no cover for its occupant (U21). Defended Obstacle (p. 45, U53) reads the same result. Consequences against the old readings: a barricade now hides a target's lower points; a model on high ground loses the U20 shortcut and the target keeps its cover only while the piece still hides part of it. Records: commands are checked against sight when they are made and events carry the results, so stored matches replay to the same states; the record's replay-compatibility number is unchanged (its definition in `RecordFormat`), and the golden records still verify. |

## Terrain combinations (checked when a piece is built)
Low and Cover: ground surface, walkable (non-walkable only if Impassable). Wall on the ground: not walkable. Wall with a High surface: walkable platform or non-walkable tower. Impassable: never walkable. Difficult and Dangerous: walkable only. High surface needs a Wall. Walkable High platforms may not overlap each other. Anything else throws ArgumentException.

## Golden scenes
Ten scenes in `tests/Rules.Tests/Scenes/*.json`, each with its written ruling and a `status`: scene 01 is confirmed (p. 30); the other nine rest on provisional readings named in their `basis`. Since line-of-sight stage L2 the scenes are ruled on the height-based sight (U77, U78); each changed scene says what it was before. Known limitation: a slit narrower than the spacing of the nine points can hide a sliver of the target the table would show (scene 04, `HeightSightTests.Test5_NarrowGapsBetweenTwoWalls`).

## Not an interpretation, but a limit
Rectangles only (no polygons; the `Footprint` type isolates this so polygons can be added later without changing movement or sight code); no general climbing, jumping or falling (tower ladders and tower sides only, U66 to U76). Charge and retreat moves were added in M4 Slice B as two optional arguments of `Movement.CheckPath` (a charge target; a retreat flag) and `Charging` (the shortest-route planner, U57 to U65); with neither argument CheckPath is unchanged from M3. Positions entered the match state in M4.
